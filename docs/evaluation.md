# Pilot Evaluation

S.A.R.A.H. was evaluated through a small-scale exploratory pilot study designed to assess whether a visual-language-model-based system could support both hazard detection and environmental assistance.

## Evaluation Setup

Three testers evaluated the prototype across a variety of indoor and outdoor environments.

Testing included locations such as:

- residential walkways
- streets
- parking areas
- parks
- mailbox areas
- stores
- indoor residential environments

Each tester evaluated the system across multiple hazard and accessibility categories.

## Evaluation Categories

The pilot assessed:

- Head-level obstacle detection
- Body-level obstacle detection
- Floor-level obstacle detection
- Landmark identification
- Voice assistant question answering
- Text reading
- False positives
- False negatives

Each category was rated on a five-point qualitative scale.

## Results

S.A.R.A.H. received ratings of at least 4/5 across all hazard-detection categories.

The system also:

- successfully identified most landmarks when asked
- answered most environmental questions correctly
- correctly read all tested text
- produced very few false negatives

The primary weakness identified during testing was false positives. The system sometimes warned users about objects that were present but not truly hazardous.

This was considered preferable to missing dangerous obstacles, but excessive warnings could reduce usability because visually impaired users rely heavily on auditory information from their surroundings.

## Limitations

This pilot was intentionally small and exploratory.

The study included only three testers and did not include visually impaired participants. The results therefore cannot establish that S.A.R.A.H. is safe or effective for visually impaired users.

The evaluation should instead be interpreted as preliminary evidence that the underlying multimodal approach is technically promising and worthy of further testing.

## Future Evaluation

Future work should include:

- larger participant samples
- testing with visually impaired participants
- quantitative hazard-detection metrics
- false-positive and false-negative rates
- response latency
- spatial localization accuracy
- usability testing
- different lighting and network conditions
